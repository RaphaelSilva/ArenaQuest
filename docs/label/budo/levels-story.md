# The story behind the levels — "o bambu do ninja"

The level curve ([levels.md](levels.md)) is built on one story. This page is the inspiration to
draw on when explaining the levels: to students on their first day, to parents, to instructors, or
when writing copy for the app. The student-facing text is in Brazilian Portuguese, the dojo's
language; adapt it freely, the meaning matters more than the words.

**A note on the legend.** It is a training folk tale, not documented history. It is most often
told with a hemp seedling (*asa*). The bamboo version is also widely told, and it is the one used
here: it carries the symbolism below, and it avoids the connotation "cânhamo" has in Brazil.

## The legend (to tell students)

> Conta-se que o aprendiz de ninja, no primeiro dia de treino, plantava uma muda de bambu. E todo
> dia, sem exceção, saltava sobre ela.
>
> No começo era fácil: o broto mal saía da terra. Mas o bambu é uma das plantas que mais cresce no
> mundo, e a cada dia estava um pouco mais alto. O aprendiz não percebia o bambu crescendo, porque
> crescia junto com ele. O salto de hoje era só um pouco maior que o de ontem.
>
> Meses depois, o aprendiz saltava sobre algo que nenhum outro conseguia. Não por um dom especial,
> mas porque nunca deixou de saltar.
>
> Aqui no dojo, cada vídeo assistido, cada módulo concluído é o salto do dia. Os níveis mostram o
> quanto o seu bambu já cresceu.

## Why bamboo

- **It grows in silence first.** Bamboo spends years building roots underground before it shoots up
  — like the first grades, where the work (ukemi, kamae, kihon) is invisible but holds everything
  that comes later.
- **It bends but does not break.** It gives way to the storm and straightens again. That is
  taijutsu: do not oppose force, flow with it.
- **Its strength is in the nodes.** Bamboo is hollow; it stands because of its nodes. Every
  difficulty overcome is a node.
- **A grove is one plant.** The canes of a bamboo grove share their roots. Whoever goes ahead
  holds up whoever comes behind.

## The stages

Each stage covers one grade being studied and has two levels (I and II). The phrase in quotes is a
suggestion for the level-up moment.

| Stage | Grade studied | What it means | Level-up phrase |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tane** — Semente | 9º Kyu | Every bamboo started as a seed. Planting it is the first jump. | "Você plantou a sua semente. O primeiro salto já foi dado." |
| **Ne** — Raiz | 8º Kyu | The roots nobody sees: the base that will hold the giant bamboo. | "Ninguém vê as raízes, mas é nelas que tudo se apoia." |
| **Takenoko** — Broto | 7º Kyu | The shoot breaks through the ground: the first visible results. | "Seu bambu rompeu a terra. Agora todos podem ver." |
| **Wakatake** — Bambu Jovem | 6º Kyu | It grows fast and is still green: energy that still needs direction. | "Cresce rápido quem treina todo dia. Continue saltando." |
| **Fushi** — Nó | 5º Kyu | The nodes are where bamboo gets its strength: every trial overcome. | "Cada nó do bambu é uma dificuldade vencida." |
| **Shinaru** — Flexível | 4º Kyu | It bends in the storm without breaking: flowing instead of resisting. | "O bambu se curva na tempestade e se levanta inteiro." |
| **Kaze** — Vento | 3º Kyu | The wind passes through the grove and the bamboo sings: movement becomes natural. | "Você já não luta contra o vento; você se move com ele." |
| **Hayashi** — Bosque | 2º Kyu | The grove shares its roots: you now hold up those who come behind. | "Um bosque é uma só planta. Ajude quem está começando." |
| **Ōtake** — Grande Bambu | 1º Kyu | Firm and tall, seen from afar: ready to be tested. | "Firme e alto. O salto final está perto." |
| **Ten** — Céu | Shodan (study) | The bamboo touches the sky: the last preparation. | "Seu bambu toca o céu." |
| **Shodan** | Programa complete | *Shodan* (初段) means "first step". | "O primeiro degrau. O caminho de verdade começa agora." |

## How to explain it in one minute

1. **The levels measure effort, not grade.** Your grade (kyu, dan) is given by your teacher in an
   exam; it is the group you belong to in the app. Your level shows how much you have trained.
2. **Every day counts.** Like the bamboo jump, progress comes from small daily repetitions. The
   level rises with each video watched and each module completed.
3. **Each stage has a lesson.** Root, node, flexibility, wind: the name of the level tells you what
   to cultivate in that phase.
4. **Shodan is the beginning.** The final level is not "the end". In Japanese, *Shodan* means first
   step, and the journey continues after it.
